Six legs, eighteen joints

18 DOF A.T.O.M. team project ROS 2 Humble · ros2_control Closed-form leg IK Docker · Gazebo Harmonic

Walking in Gazebo

A six-legged walker doing a tripod gait, driven live from the keyboard. A /cmd_vel command goes in; the gait engine turns that body velocity into six foot trajectories, and the leg IK turns each foot position into three joint angles. Eighteen in all.

This clip is the Gazebo simulation. The same control node runs on the physical robot.

Built with A.T.O.M.

This is a team robot, built with the A.T.O.M. robotics lab at MAIT, not a solo project. Three parts of it are mine.

  • The control node. A /cmd_vel command becomes a tripod gait through ros2_control, driving all eighteen joints. Two triangles of legs alternate between stance and swing, so three feet are always on the ground.
  • The leg inverse kinematics. A closed-form, law-of-cosines solution that turns each foot target into coxa, femur and tibia angles. It replaced a hand-tuned angle table — the table worked for the poses somebody had tuned and nothing else, so the robot could only walk the way it had been taught rather than the way it was commanded.
  • The environment. I containerised the ROS 2 Humble and Gazebo Harmonic stack in Docker, with GPU and X11 passthrough so the simulator renders from inside the container. Before that, a build that worked on one laptop was no evidence it would work on anyone else's.

Why closed-form and not a solver

A general IK solver would work here and would be the wrong tool. A three-joint leg has a closed-form solution you can write down with the law of cosines: given a foot position, the angles fall out of two triangles, and it either has a solution or the target is out of reach.

That matters at gait rates. Eighteen joints resolved every control cycle, six legs at a time, on a robot that has to stay standing while it thinks. A closed-form solution runs in constant time and never fails to converge, because there is nothing to converge.

The physical build

  • 624.6 mm leg-tip span, designed in Fusion 360.
  • DS3225MG 25 kg servos at every joint.
  • 15×24×5 mm bearings at the joints so the printed plastic is not carrying the load.

CAD file

Six legs of three links each, dimensioned at 624.6 × 247.3 mm in front elevation. The project folder holds a file honestly named "zamin pe chalne wala keeda", a bug that walks on the ground. Accurate.
