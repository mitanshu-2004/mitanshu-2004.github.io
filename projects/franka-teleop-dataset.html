Teleop data → a π0.5 policy

π0.5 VLA 10 tasks CS66 + FR3 125 Hz teleop LeRobot format

Driving the FR3 by hand

I teleoperate a Franka FR3 through a manipulation task. Here it is stacking rings on a peg. Every run is recorded as one episode, with its camera frames and joint readings, and the pile of episodes becomes a dataset a policy can learn from.

The point is to capture how a person actually solves the task, mistakes and all. A policy trained only on clean, optimal demonstrations has never seen a recovery, so it has no idea what to do the first time something slips.

Controller to arm, in RViz

Behind the demo: the controller's pose drives the arm through SE(3) transforms, mirrored live in RViz. Watching the model track the controller is how I check the mapping before anything moves the real FR3.

This is also where sign errors surface. A flipped axis looks obvious in RViz and expensive on the real arm, so nothing reaches hardware until the mirror behaves.

The loop that collects it

  • The same real-time C++ loop I wrote for the CS66 pair drives this arm: Cartesian servoing at 125 Hz, Quest 3 hand pose in over UDP.
  • Poses the headset cannot vouch for are discarded before they reach the arm.
  • The same safety clamps sit between me and this arm as on the CS66 pair, which matters more here: a bad episode is not just a bad run, it is a bad run that ends up in the training set.
  • Ten tasks were collected across both platforms rather than one. A policy that has only ever seen a single arm tends to fit the quirks of that arm's geometry along with the task.

Training the policy

I fine-tuned a π0.5 vision-language-action policy on the 10-task set and worked on its inference path.

Collecting demonstrations and never training on them leaves the interesting half of the question unanswered. You do not find out whether your data is good until something tries to learn from it, and the failures point straight back at the collection: a task the policy cannot do is usually a task you recorded badly.

Everything is written in LeRobot's dataset format, so it drops into standard imitation-learning training without a conversion step of my own invention sitting in the middle.

What I would fix

  • Ten tasks is a small set. It is enough to fine-tune on and not enough to make strong claims about generalisation, and I would rather say that than imply otherwise.
  • Episode counts per task were never logged in a form I can quote now. I should have decided what the collection script wrote out before I started collecting, rather than after.
