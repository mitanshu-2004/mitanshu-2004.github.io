Bodhi — the humanoid that answers

UBTech Yanshee YOLOv8 NeMo ASR Wake-word routing

Ask it, and it answers out loud

You say Bodhi's wake word, ask a question, and it looks at what is in front of it and answers out loud. The clip has sound, and the reply you hear is the robot's own.

It runs on a UBTech Yanshee, a small hobby-grade humanoid. Turn the sound on.

How it works

  • Vision is a custom-trained YOLOv8 detector running on the Yanshee's MJPEG stream, so the robot names what it sees instead of guessing from context.
  • Speech comes in through an NVIDIA NeMo ASR pipeline. A wake word gates it, and what follows gets routed — a question goes to a chat service, a scan request goes to a QR reader.
  • Getting the routing right mattered more than either model. Without a wake word the robot answers every stray sentence in a noisy room; with a push-to-talk button it stops being something you speak to at all.

Working through a phone-grade camera

The Yanshee streams MJPEG, which is a series of independently compressed frames over HTTP. It is easy to consume and it is not a good video pipeline: no interframe compression, visible artefacts, and latency that varies with the network rather than with anything you control.

That shapes the detector. A model tuned on clean images does poorly on a soft, compressed frame from a robot that is also moving, so I trained against frames pulled from the robot's own stream rather than a clean dataset.

The hardware is the boring part

The Yanshee is roughly the size of a large doll and costs about as much as a laptop. Nothing about the servos or the camera is research-grade, and none of my work went into them. It went into tying the detector, the ASR, the routing and the reply into one loop that behaves sensibly when a stranger walks up and starts talking.
